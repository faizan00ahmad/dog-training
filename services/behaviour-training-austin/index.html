Dog behaviour training and reactivity help in Austin, TX

Lunging at other dogs. Barking at the doorbell. Growling over a bone. These aren't bad dogs. They're dogs struggling with something, and we find out what before we change anything.

Problems we help with

  • Leash reactivity. Barking, lunging or spinning when other dogs, bikes or people pass. Start with our leash reactivity guide.
  • Barking. Doorbell, visitors, windows, delivery drivers. See six fixes for door barking.
  • Resource guarding of food, toys, beds or people.
  • Separation anxiety and distress when left alone. Read anxiety or boredom?
  • Fear and nervousness around strangers, noises or new places.
  • Jumping up, nipping and over-excitement.

Why we start with an assessment

The same behaviour can come from very different causes. A dog barking at visitors might be excited, protective or scared, and each needs a different plan. So the first visit is a proper look: your dog's history, their body language, the triggers and the layout of your home.

If we suspect pain or a medical problem, we'll ask you to check with your vet before going any further. Behaviour and health are closely linked.

What you can expect

  1. Free 15-minute call to decide whether we're the right fit. If your case is beyond us (for example, serious aggression) we'll say so and point you to a veterinary behaviourist.
  2. In-home assessment and first session, with a written plan.
  3. Coaching on real triggers in your home and on your walks.
  4. Follow-up support by phone and email between visits.

Training is reward-based and paced to your dog. We never push a dog past what they can handle, and we never use fear, pain or intimidation.

Pricing

An assessment plus first session starts at $150. Behaviour cases often benefit from follow-up sessions or a package, and we'll give you an honest estimate after the first visit.

Behaviour Training FAQs

Can you help with an aggressive dog?

We help with many reactive and fearful dogs. If a dog has bitten or is likely to cause serious harm, we'll refer you to a veterinary behaviourist and work alongside them where appropriate.

Why is my dog only reactive on a leash?

A leash stops a dog from moving away or greeting normally, which can create fear or frustration. Reward-based desensitisation helps.

Will my dog need medication?

Sometimes. That's a decision for your vet. We'll tell you if we think a vet visit is worth it.

How long will behaviour training take?

It depends on the dog and the problem. Many families see progress within weeks, though deeply rooted fears take longer.
