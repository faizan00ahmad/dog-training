Dog training FAQs for Austin owners

Answers to what people ask before they book: methods, prices, puppies, behaviour and how it all works.

About our training

Should the whole family come to the session?

Ideally, yes. Everyone who handles the dog should use the same cues. If that isn't possible, one person can attend and we'll write a plan to share.

Do you train reactive dogs at home?

Yes. Reactive dogs are a big part of the work. We keep your dog below the point where they react and never push past what they can handle.

How many sessions will my dog need?

Many households see a big change after one two-hour session. Complex cases may need a few follow-ups. We'll tell you honestly after the first visit.

What if it rains or I need to reschedule?

We move outdoor work under cover or indoors where we can. Give us 24 hours' notice to reschedule for free.

Do you offer refunds?

Training depends on practice at home, so we can't promise a fixed result. If you're unhappy after the first month, we'll change the plan or refund unused sessions.

What training methods do you use?

Reward-based, force-free methods only. We use treats, toys and praise, and never prong, choke or shock collars.

Are you certified and insured?

Alex Morgan holds the CPDT-KA credential and we carry liability insurance and bonding for in-home work.

Pricing and booking

How much does dog training cost in Austin?

Private in-home sessions across Austin generally run $100 to $200. Ours start at $110 for puppies, $120 for obedience and $150 for a behaviour assessment plus session. See our full price guide.

Is there a travel fee?

No travel fee within Austin, Round Rock, Cedar Park and Pflugerville. We'll tell you before booking if you're outside our area.

How do I book?

Book a free 15-minute call using the form on our contact page, or phone us. We'll recommend the best first step.

Puppies and behaviour

When should I start puppy training?

From the day you bring your puppy home, usually around 8 weeks.

Can you help with a dog that bites?

If a dog has bitten, we start with a free call and may refer you to a veterinary behaviourist. Safety comes first.

My dog barks at everything. Can you help?

Yes. We find out why they bark and teach a calmer alternative. See our guide to door barking.
