In-home dog training vs group classes: how to choose

Both formats work. They just solve different problems. Here's how to match your dog to the right one before you spend a cent.

By Alex Morgan, CPDT-KA2026-10-136 min read

People ask us this almost every week: "Should we do a class, or have someone come to the house?" It's a fair question, and the honest answer is that neither is better. They're good at different things.

What group classes do well

A decent group class gives your dog practice being calm around other dogs and people. That's hard to replicate at home. It's also the cheapest way to cover basics like sit, down, name response and polite greetings.

  • Brilliant for puppies who need safe exposure to other puppies.
  • Good for confident, friendly adult dogs who just need manners.
  • Budget-friendly. You're splitting the trainer's time with several families.

Where classes fall down is that the dog is learning in a distracting room, with a stranger, once a week. Plenty of dogs can do everything perfectly at class and still pull your arm off the moment you step onto your own street.

What in-home training does well

Private in-home training starts from the actual problem. If your dog barks when the delivery driver arrives, we're there when the delivery driver arrives. If the couch is a battleground, we work on the couch.

  • The trainer sees your dog's real behaviour, not their best-behaviour-at-a-new-place version.
  • The whole household can take part, including kids who'd never sit through an evening class.
  • Reactive, anxious or older dogs aren't pushed into a room full of strangers.
  • The plan is built around your routine, your layout and your walking routes.

A quick way to decide

Your situationBetter fit
New puppy, no serious problemsGroup class, plus one private session for house rules
Dog pulls hard on walksIn-home (we practise on your real route)
Dog lunges, growls or panics near other dogsIn-home first. Classes come later, if at all
Barking at visitors, doorbell or windowsIn-home
Rescue dog settling inIn-home, for the first weeks
Confident dog who just needs polishGroup class

You can do both

This is what we suggest most often for puppies. Start with one private session so house training, biting and crate routines are sorted at home. Then add a small group class once your puppy has had their vaccinations, so the socialising happens when it's safe and useful.

Questions to ask either way

  • Which methods and equipment do you use? (Reward-based is the standard worth looking for.)
  • How many dogs are in a class? Six or fewer is far better than twelve.
  • Do you follow up after the lesson?
  • What's your approach if my dog gets overwhelmed?

Not sure which suits your dog? Tell us what's going on on a free 15-minute call and we'll point you to the right format, even if that's a class somewhere else.

Quick answers

Is private dog training worth it?

For specific behaviour problems or home-based issues, usually yes. You get a plan designed around your dog and your environment, and progress tends to be faster.

Can I do both a group class and private training?

Yes. Many owners start with a private session and add a small class for socialisation once their puppy is fully vaccinated.

Is a group class good for a reactive dog?

Usually not at first. Reactive dogs generally do better with private sessions until they have the skills to cope around other dogs.
