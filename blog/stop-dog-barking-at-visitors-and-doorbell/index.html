Why does my dog bark at visitors and the doorbell?

Door-barking is the number one complaint we hear. The fix isn't telling your dog to be quiet. It's giving them a better thing to do.

By Alex Morgan, CPDT-KA2026-11-107 min read

The doorbell rings, your dog launches, the visitor's already uncomfortable, and you're shouting "quiet!" over a wall of noise. If that's your evening, this one's for you.

Why dogs bark at the door

For most dogs, it's some mix of excitement, alert and "this is my territory." The doorbell predicts something big, so the dog gets ready for it. Barking works, too, since the person usually leaves or comes in, and the dog feels like they did something.

That's why shouting rarely helps. To your dog, you joining in sounds like more barking.

Fix 1: Teach a different job

Pick a spot, a mat or bed away from the door. Teach your dog to go there and lie down on cue, using treats. Practise without the doorbell first. Then add it, slowly.

Fix 2: Break the doorbell association

Ring the bell (or play a recording), give a treat the second it sounds. Do this dozens of times, calmly. Soon the bell predicts a treat, not a stampede.

Fix 3: Manage the first few seconds

Practise opening the door while your dog is on their mat, with a family member helping. If your dog breaks, close the door. Repeat. They learn that calm gets the door to open and barking closes it.

Fix 4: Block the view

If your dog barks at people passing the window, window film or a closed blind takes away the trigger. It isn't cheating. Management is part of training because every rehearsal strengthens the habit.

Fix 5: Exercise and enrichment

A bored, under-exercised dog finds the doorbell the highlight of the day. A sniffing walk, a food puzzle or a short training game before visitors arrive can lower the volume surprisingly well.

Fix 6: Plan guest arrivals

For the first weeks, put your dog behind a gate or in another room with a chew when guests come. Let them out after the visitors settle. It isn't forever. It stops your dog from practising the bad version.

Things to avoid

  • Shouting or spraying water.
  • Anti-bark collars. They can punish fear, making the underlying problem worse.
  • Letting guests give frantic greetings. Ask them to ignore the dog until things calm.

When barking is about fear

Some dogs bark at visitors because they're scared. If your dog backs away while barking, tucks their tail, or growls when someone gets close, it's more than annoyance. A trainer should help you build comfort, not just quiet. Our in-home behaviour training works with your real doorbell and your real hallway.

Quick win: keep a jar of treats near the door and drop one on the mat every time the doorbell rings. Within a week many dogs start heading for the mat on their own.

Quick answers

How do I stop my dog barking when the doorbell rings?

Teach a mat or bed cue, pair the doorbell with treats, and practise opening the door in small steps. Rewarding calm works better than shouting.

Do anti-bark collars work?

They may suppress barking temporarily, but they punish rather than teach and can increase fear. Reward-based training changes the habit.

Why does my dog bark at people walking past the window?

It's often territorial or excitement-based, and barking feels successful when people walk away. Blocking the view and teaching a calm alternative helps.
