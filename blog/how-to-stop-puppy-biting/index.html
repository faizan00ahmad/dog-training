How to stop puppy biting: what works at 8 to 16 weeks

Those needle teeth are normal, and they do pass. Here's how to speed things up and save your sleeves.

By Alex Morgan, CPDT-KA2026-11-037 min read

Your new puppy is adorable for about eleven seconds, and then they latch onto your sleeve like a tiny alligator. Welcome to puppyhood. Biting is one of the most common reasons we get called to new puppy homes, and the encouraging news is that it's both normal and fixable.

Why puppies bite

Puppies explore with their mouths. They're also teething from around 12 weeks, so chewing feels good. And they play with their littermates by mouthing, so biting you is just how they say "let's play." It isn't aggression, and it doesn't mean you've got a problem dog.

Most puppies bite far less by 5 or 6 months, once their adult teeth are in. Until then, you can teach them how to use their mouth gently.

What to do in the moment

  1. Stay calm. Squealing and waving your hands often makes puppies bite harder because it looks like fun.
  2. Say "ouch" once, then stop moving. If your puppy keeps biting, calmly stand up, or step out of the room for 20 seconds. You're teaching them that biting makes the fun end.
  3. Offer something they can bite. A chew toy, a stuffed rubber toy, a rope. Praise when they chew the right thing.
  4. Repeat. It feels slow. It works.

Prevent the bites you can see coming

  • Tired puppies bite more. Overtired pups get "witching hour" zoomies. A nap in a crate or pen helps.
  • Hungry puppies bite more. Check meal timing and use part of their food in toys.
  • Keep toys everywhere. If a toy is within reach, it's easier to redirect.
  • Don't play rough with hands. Use a tug toy so your hands never become the toy.

Teach "soft mouth"

This sounds fancy but it's simple. When your puppy mouths you gently, reward. When they bite hard, pause the game. Over a week or two they learn that gentle gets rewarded and hard doesn't. The goal isn't zero mouthing right away. It's gradual control.

Things that don't help

  • Holding the puppy's mouth shut or pinning them down.
  • Spraying them with water or yelling.
  • Tapping their nose. It can make a puppy fearful of hands.

Those approaches don't teach what to do, and they can damage trust right when you need it most.

When biting isn't just play

Speak to a trainer if your puppy growls when guarded, bites in a way that breaks skin repeatedly, or seems to get worse rather than better after a few weeks. A quick session will tell you whether it's ordinary puppyhood or something that needs a closer look. Remember: puppy teeth are sharp because puppies need to learn bite control before their jaws get strong. You're not failing. You're teaching.

Quick answers

When do puppies stop biting?

Most puppies bite far less by 5 to 6 months as adult teeth come in, especially with consistent training and enough chew options.

Is it normal for my 8 week old puppy to bite so much?

Yes. Mouthing and nipping are typical at this age. Consistent redirection and rest are the best tools.

Should I punish my puppy for biting?

No. Punishment can create fear. Redirect to a toy, pause play, and reward gentle behaviour instead.
