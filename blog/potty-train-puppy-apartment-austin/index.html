How to potty train a puppy in an apartment: a 7-day plan

Elevators, balconies and shared lawns make apartment potty training trickier. A simple routine makes it very doable.

By Alex Morgan, CPDT-KA2026-11-058 min read

Apartment life makes house training a bit more of a project. There's the elevator, the walk to the patch of grass, the neighbour on the stairs. But it's completely doable, and most puppies pick it up quickly if the routine is consistent.

The basics that never change

  • Puppies need to go out far more often than you think. A rough guide is one hour per month of age, plus a little extra, but young pups often can't wait that long.
  • Take them out after waking, after eating, after play and before bed.
  • Use the same spot, the same word ("go potty"), and reward right after they go.
  • Never punish accidents. It teaches them to hide, not to wait.

Set up before the puppy arrives

  • A crate sized for sleeping. Big enough to stand and turn around, but not big enough to use one corner as a bathroom.
  • An enzymatic cleaner. Regular cleaners leave a scent that invites repeat accidents.
  • Treats at the door. Keep them in a jar by the lead so you can reward fast.
  • A routes plan. Know your quickest way from the door to grass. In a tower block, that speed matters.

The 7-day plan

Days 1 and 2: Build the routine

Take your puppy out every 60 to 90 minutes when awake. Stay with them until they go. Praise and treat the moment they finish. If nothing happens in five minutes, go back in, keep them with you, and try again in ten.

Days 3 and 4: Watch the signs

Sniffing, circling and sudden quiet are signals. Learn them. Many accidents are just missed hints. Keep your puppy tethered to you or in a pen when you can't watch.

Days 5 and 6: Stretch the gaps

If there have been no accidents for two days, stretch the time between trips by 15 minutes. Keep rewards generous.

Day 7: Review and adjust

Count the accidents. A few is normal. If there are many, shorten the gaps again. House training isn't a test you pass in a week. It's a habit that firms up.

Nights and work days

Young puppies often need one or two night trips. Keep them boring: quiet voice, lights low, straight out and straight back. For long work days, arrange a midday break with a neighbour, a walker or a puppy day care. An 8-week-old puppy can't wait eight hours.

When it isn't going to plan

  • Accidents right after coming in: your puppy probably got distracted outside. Stay with them and wait for the job to be done.
  • Regression after a week of success: normal. Go back one step.
  • Lots of urination or straining: talk to your vet. Sometimes it's a medical issue, not training.

Tip for hot weeks: Austin summers are hard on tiny paws. Check the pavement with the back of your hand and take earlier or later trips.

Quick answers

How long does it take to house train a puppy in an apartment?

Many puppies are reliable in about 4 to 8 weeks with consistent routines, though full reliability often takes a few months.

How often should I take my puppy out?

Every 60 to 90 minutes when awake, and always after waking, eating, playing and before bed, then stretch the gaps as they succeed.

What if my puppy has an accident in the crate?

Clean it with an enzymatic cleaner, shorten the time between trips, and check the crate size. Don't punish.
