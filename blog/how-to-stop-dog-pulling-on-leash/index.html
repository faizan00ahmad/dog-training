How to stop your dog pulling on the leash

Pulling isn't stubbornness. It works, so your dog keeps doing it. Here's how to make walking beside you the better deal.

By Alex Morgan, CPDT-KA2026-10-208 min read

Your dog isn't being dominant when they drag you down the street. They're just in a hurry to get somewhere good, and pulling has worked every single time. The trick is to make it stop working.

Why dogs pull in the first place

Dogs walk faster than we do, they're excited, and the world smells incredible. Every time they pull and you follow, the leash teaches them that tension means progress. Over a few months that becomes a very strong habit.

So the fix is simple in theory: pulling gets nowhere, and a loose leash gets everything the dog wants. It's the doing that takes practice.

Before you head out: set up for success

  • Use a well-fitted harness or flat collar. A front-clip harness can reduce the leverage a dog has. Avoid prong, choke and shock collars, which suppress the behaviour without teaching the dog anything.
  • Use a 4 to 6 foot leash. Not a retractable one. Retractables teach dogs that constant tension is normal.
  • Bring small, soft treats. Something your dog actually loves, not their regular kibble.
  • Practise somewhere boring first. Your hallway, driveway or backyard, before the busy park.

Step 1: Reward the position you want

Stand still with your dog at your side. Every time they look at you or stay close, say "yes" and drop a treat right next to your leg. You're teaching them that this spot is where the good stuff arrives. Do it for a few minutes a day until your dog starts checking in without being asked.

Step 2: Walk a few steps and pay

Take three or four steps. If the leash is loose, mark with "yes" and treat beside your leg. If your dog moves ahead, stop. Wait for them to come back or glance at you, then reward and walk again. At first you'll go nowhere. That's normal.

Step 3: Stop being a tree, and start being a turn

If your dog surges ahead and the leash goes tight, turn the other way calmly and walk off. When they catch up and the leash loosens, reward them. You're not yanking or scolding. You're just changing direction so pulling doesn't get them closer to the thing they wanted.

Step 4: Give them a job, not just a rule

Most dogs are happier with something to do. Let them sniff on a cue ("go sniff") after a stretch of good walking. Sniffing is a reward in itself, and it means a walk is a conversation, not a drill.

Mistakes that keep dogs pulling

  • Following the pull "just this once." Once is all it takes to reinforce it.
  • Going too fast too soon. Practise with low distraction before expecting calm near squirrels or other dogs.
  • Short, tired-owner sessions. A rushed ten minutes before work often undoes the progress. Do a proper five-minute training walk and a separate sniffy one.
  • Using tools to force it. A harness can help, but it doesn't teach a dog what to do instead.
  • Training in the heat. Austin pavement in August is no place for focus. Walk early, or train indoors.

When to call a trainer

If your dog pulls so hard you're afraid of falling, if they lunge at other dogs, or if you've practised for three weeks with no change, get help. Often a trainer will spot something you can't see, like your timing or the route you've chosen. Our obedience training sessions are done on your actual street so the skills carry over.

Rule of thumb: if you can't give your dog 100% of your attention for the first few weeks of loose-leash training, reduce the distractions rather than your standards.

Quick answers

How long does it take to teach a dog not to pull?

With daily practice, most dogs show clear improvement within two to four weeks. Strong habits and exciting environments can take longer.

Is a harness or collar better for a dog that pulls?

A well-fitted front-clip harness often reduces pulling force, but the lasting fix is teaching the dog that a loose leash is rewarded.

Should I use a retractable leash?

Not for training. They encourage constant tension and give you less control near traffic or other dogs.
